CoffeeLab Journal — Privacy Policy

Last updated 2 September 2026

The short version. CoffeeLab Journal is a personal coffee journal. Everything you log — your coffees, ratings, tasting notes, photos and brews — stays on your device and in your own iCloud account. I cannot see any of it.

There is exactly one thing the app can send me, and only if you switch it on: an anonymous summary of your taste. It is off by default, you can read the exact data before you agree, and you can delete it at any time from inside the app.

Who is responsible

The data controller for this app is Ömer Tarik Özyilmaz, an individual developer. You can reach me about anything on this page, including any request about your data, at otozyilmaz@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

Almost all of it. The app is built around a local database that syncs through your own iCloud account using Apple's CloudKit private database. Data in a private database is accessible only to you — as the developer I have no technical means of reading it, and I never receive a copy.

This includes:

  • Your coffees, roasters, origins, processes, roast dates and prices
  • Your ratings, tasting notes, photos and videos
  • Every brew you log, including recipes, timings and equipment
  • Your caffeine log and any drinks you record while out
  • Your achievements, streaks and settings

If you delete the app or turn off iCloud, that data goes with it. You can export a full backup file at any time from Settings.

Health data

With your permission, the app reads sleep from Apple Health so it can compare its caffeine projections against how you actually slept, and writes the caffeine from drinks you log back into Health. This exchange happens entirely on your device between the app and Apple Health. Health data is never transmitted anywhere, is not included in the optional taste sharing described below, and you can revoke access at any time in the Health app. The app works without Health access.

The one thing you can choose to share

The app's distinctive feature is a taste vector: a standardised summary of what your ratings say you like. It is computed on your device from your own library.

If — and only if — you turn on Contribute my ratings in Settings, the app sends that summary to a server I operate, so that palates can be grouped into archetypes and the app can eventually tell you what people with your taste also enjoy.

Exactly what is sent

FieldWhat it is
The vectorA number per flavour family, process, origin region and roast level, saying how much more or less than your own average you rate that category
Support countsHow many coffees back each number, so thin evidence can be weighted down
Rated countHow many coffees you have rated in total

Install identifierA random identifier generated on your device (see below)
App versionWhich build produced the vector

You can see the literal data before deciding: Settings → Share ratings → Show me the payload displays the exact bytes that would be sent.

What is never sent

  • Your name, email address, Apple ID or any account identifier
  • Coffee names, roaster names, your tasting notes, photos or videos
  • Your individual ratings — only the standardised summary derived from them
  • Your location, the cafés you visit, or any device identifier
  • Any health or sleep data
  • Your brew logs, timings or caffeine history

The install identifier

The summary is submitted with a random identifier created on your device the first time it is needed. It is not derived from your hardware, your Apple ID, or Apple's advertising or vendor identifiers, and it is not shared with anyone. Its only purpose is to let the server replace your previous summary rather than counting your palate twice.

When you turn sharing off, or use the delete button, the identifier is discarded and a new one generated. Anything you choose to share afterwards therefore cannot be linked to what you shared before.

Legal basis, and taking it back

The lawful basis for this processing is your consent under Article 6(1)(a) GDPR. Consent is entirely optional: the app is fully functional without it, and nothing is withheld from you for declining.

You can withdraw at any time in Settings → Share ratings, either by switching the toggle off or by tapping Delete what I've shared. Either action deletes your summary from the server. Withdrawing is as easy as giving consent, and does not affect the lawfulness of processing before you withdrew.

How long it is kept

The server holds one summary per install, replaced whenever your taste meaningfully changes — not a history. It is kept until you delete it or until the research it supports ends, after which the data set is deleted. No automated decision-making or profiling with legal effects is performed on it.

Optional AI features

The app can explain its predictions in plain language. By default this runs entirely on your device using Apple's built-in on-device model, and nothing is transmitted.

You may optionally supply your own API key for Anthropic or OpenAI to use a cloud model instead. If you do, the text of your question and a summary of your taste are sent directly from your device to that provider under your own account, governed by their privacy policy — not mine. I do not receive that traffic, and I never see your key, which is stored in your device's Keychain. This is off unless you enter a key yourself.

Importing a coffee from a web address

If you paste a roaster's product page into the app to fill in a coffee's details, your device fetches that page directly from that roaster's website. That website will see the request as it would any visit. Nothing about you or your library is sent to it.

Beta testing through TestFlight

While the app is distributed through TestFlight, Apple processes some data about your participation, and as the developer I can see it in App Store Connect:

  • The email address you used to accept the TestFlight invitation
  • Crash reports and basic engagement metrics such as session counts
  • Any feedback or screenshots you choose to submit through TestFlight

This is separate from anything the app itself sends, and is governed by Apple's privacy policy. I use it only to fix problems and to contact testers about the beta. I do not combine it with taste summaries — and could not, since those carry no identifier that could be matched to a person.

What this app does not do

  • No advertising and no ad networks.
  • No analytics or tracking SDKs. There is no third-party analytics code in the app at all.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no data is shared with data brokers.
  • Nothing is sold. Not now, and a change of that kind would require asking you again.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict and port your data, and to object to processing. Because virtually everything the app holds lives on your own device and in your own iCloud account, you already exercise most of these directly: you can view, edit, export and delete any of it in the app at any time.

For the taste summary, the delete button in Settings is an immediate erasure. If you would prefer to ask me instead, or want to exercise any other right, write to otozyilmaz@gmail.com and I will respond within one month.

If you believe I have handled your data improperly, you may complain to your national data protection authority. In the Netherlands this is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

Children

This app is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect any data from them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that affects what leaves your device, the date at the top will change and the app will ask for your consent again rather than relying on the consent you gave to an earlier version.
